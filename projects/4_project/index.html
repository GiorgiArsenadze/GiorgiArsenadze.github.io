Particles on the Rotating Channels in the Wormhole Metrics

In this paper, I investigated the motion of particles along curved, rotating trajectories within the Ellis-Bronnikov wormhole metrics.

\[ds^2 = -dt^2 + dl^2 + (b_0^2 + l^2)(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2)\]

I derived the reduced 1+1-dimensional metric and the corresponding Lagrangian for a free particle:

\[L = -\frac{1}{2} g_{\alpha \beta} \frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau} \frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}.\]

I solved the equations of motion both analytically and numerically, finding that under certain conditions, particles moving along twisted, lagging channels can asymptotically reach infinity, effectively leaving the wormhole—a behavior not possible for straight co-rotating trajectories. This analysis highlighted how curvature and rotation influence particle dynamics in exotic geometries.

I extended previous studies on particle dynamics in rotating magnetic fields by examining more realistic, curvilinear field lines, such as Archimedean spirals described by:

\[\phi(l) = a l, \quad \theta(l) = \theta_0\]

where (a) is the spiral parameter. I showed that particles can achieve a force-free regime, allowing them to escape the wormhole interior. I also explored three-dimensional trajectories, demonstrating that the exact particle path depends sensitively on initial conditions and the geometry of the rotating channels. These results reveal the complex interplay between geometry, rotation, and particle motion in relativistic settings.

Through this work, I developed a deeper understanding of how theoretical wormhole models can inform astrophysical phenomena such as plasma behavior near pulsars or supermassive black holes. The study also strengthened my analytical and computational skills, particularly in solving nonlinear equations of motion and simulating particle trajectories. Overall, this research enhanced my ability to tackle complex problems in theoretical physics while providing transferable skills in modeling, numerical analysis, and problem-solving in dynamic systems.
