## Particle Motion in Rotating Channels of Wormhole Metrics

In this work, I investigated the motion of particles along curved, rotating trajectories within the Ellis-Bronnikov wormhole metrics. I derived the reduced 1+1-dimensional metric for a particle moving along a prescribed curve, given by:

$$
ds^2 = -dt^2 + dl^2
$$

where \(l\) is the proper distance along the curve. Using this, I constructed the corresponding Lagrangian:

$$
L = -\frac{1}{2} g_{\alpha\beta} \frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau} \frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}
$$

and solved the equations of motion analytically and numerically. I found that particles moving along twisted, lagging channels can asymptotically reach infinity, effectively leaving the wormhole—a phenomenon not possible for straight co-rotating trajectories.

I extended previous studies of particle dynamics in rotating magnetic fields by considering curvilinear field lines, such as Archimedean spirals:

$$
\phi(l) = a l + b
$$

I showed that in these configurations, particles can reach a force-free regime, allowing them to escape the wormhole interior. I also explored three-dimensional cases, demonstrating that particle trajectories strongly depend on initial conditions and the geometry of the rotating channels.

This research allowed me to connect theoretical models of wormholes with astrophysical phenomena, such as plasma motion near pulsars and supermassive black holes. It also helped me develop advanced analytical and computational skills, including solving nonlinear equations of motion and simulating complex particle trajectories. Overall, this work strengthened my ability to tackle challenging problems in theoretical physics and provided transferable skills in modeling, numerical analysis, and problem-solving in complex dynamical systems.
